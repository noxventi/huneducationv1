In brief

Why should you study in Hungary?

Studying at a university in Hungary is one of the most accessible routes to a degree from an EU member state, taught entirely in English. Universities do not ask for a national entrance exam score; each faculty runs its own assessment. Tuition and living costs together come to €8,500 – €14,000 for an academic year. Long-established Hungarian universities such as Semmelweis, Debrecen, Pécs and Szeged teach medicine, engineering, business, master’s degrees and pilot training in English.

Hun Education has focused on this one country since 1999. We match students to the right option among 20 universities and 490 English-taught programmes. We go through current university fees in Hungary and the admission requirements with you before you apply. The full case for the country, with the figures behind it, is on the Why Hungary? page.

    Ask Another Question

    Hungarian universities run their own application and admission process, and a national entrance exam score is generally not an admission requirement. But that does not mean “admission without an exam”: many programmes set an entrance exam of their own:

    Health programmes set a chemistry and biology exam (oral or written), engineering an online physics and mathematics exam, architecture physics, mathematics and a portfolio, and film and design programmes a portfolio.

    Documents required: the application form, a passport copy, a transcript in English or certified translation, an apostilled diploma, a six-month bank statement and an English CV (Europass preferred).

    For recognition of your degree after graduation you also need to check the current rules of the competent authority in your own country.

    Most universities run their own interview or online test rather than ask for a certificate. Where one is asked for, bachelor’s programmes want an IELTS score of 5, 6 or 6.5 and master’s programmes 6.5 or an equivalent.

    If you are not at B2 you can apply to the universities’ own English Language Preparatory programmes, which start from €2,500 a year. The accepted certificate and the minimum score vary by university and by programme.

    Hungary has two intakes a year:

    September (autumn) intake: most universities start in September, so applications are expected by April, May or June.
    February (spring) intake: you need to apply by the end of October, and November at the latest.

    Exact dates depend on each university’s own academic calendar, and an intake can close early once places run out.

    Tuition and living costs together run €8,500 – €14,000 a year. Tuition is €3,000–5,000 for a bachelor’s, €4,000–6,000 for a master’s and €15,800 to $19,900 for medicine and dentistry. Living costs alone come to €4,000–8,000 a year.

    Accommodation is the most volatile item: university dormitories cost €60–400 a month, a rented room around €350 and a studio flat around €550. On top of that come roughly €300 a month in living costs, €120 for transport, €300 a year for health insurance, plus a one-off €140 application fee and a €95–145 visa fee.

    The figures change with the university, the city and how you live, and universities can update them. You can build your own breakdown in the cost section above.

    The visa decision rests entirely with the official authority; no consultancy can guarantee a visa. If the visa is refused, tuition already paid is refunded within 30 working days; application and entrance exam fees are not refunded.

    Using a degree earned abroad in your own country normally requires a recognition assessment by the competent national authority. Recognition is judged on the university’s standing, the content of the programme, the length of study and the graduation conditions, and some fields require an additional exam (for example a placement test).

    Because the rules change, review your national authority’s current recognition regulations before you apply. We make no claim of automatic or guaranteed recognition.
